About me

I’m an AI research engineer working on healthcare applications and biomedical signal analysis, with a background in computational neuroscience. I hold an MSc in computational neuroscience from the Brain and Cognition programme at Pompeu Fabra University.

I’m currently an AI research engineer intern in the Digital Health Unit at the Barcelona Supercomputing Center, where I benchmark an ECG foundation model against machine-learning models and CNN architectures trained from scratch for myocardial infarction detection. The work uses a private Hospital de Sant Pau cohort and a shared evaluation protocol on BSC’s high-performance computing infrastructure.

I apply deep learning to large-scale neural and cardiac datasets, and I have worked with fMRI, EEG, ECG, EMG, and LFP data across the full research pipeline: literature review, method selection, data auditing and preprocessing, model development and evaluation, and deployment as usable services. My broader technical background also includes one year of full-stack software development and experience building LLM-based agentic AI systems with FastAPI, Streamlit, Docker, AWS, and LangChain.

For my master’s thesis, supervised by Prof. Gustavo Deco and Prof. Rubén Moreno-Bote, I explored how the brain navigates a dynamic landscape of states in vortex space using 7T resting-state fMRI. I investigated whether richer brain-state repertoires and more flexible transitions are linked to better cognition.

Prior to my master’s, I completed a bachelor’s degree in industrial engineering at Sharif University of Technology, where I focused on the application of reinforcement learning for inventory optimization.

Master's Thesis - Brain States in Vortex Space
Dec 2025 – Jul 2026
  • Tested whether a richer, more flexible brain-state repertoire in vortex space is associated with better cognition and better modelled state transitions, using 7T resting-state fMRI from 182 Human Connectome Project participants (900 volumes each; TR 1 s; Schaefer parcellation coarsened to 100 cortical regions).
  • Built a seven-state synchronization atlas in MATLAB using a local Kuramoto order parameter, 50 principal components (73.9% variance) and k-means over 163,800 pooled volumes; adjusted Rand index reached 0.82 across resampled time points and 0.64 across independent participant halves.
  • Implemented and evaluated Maximum Occupancy Principle models testing whether transitions favour states that preserve access to more future states.
Key Findings
Across 28 planned tests of seven cognitive outcomes, transition and occupancy entropy associations were positive in 26 of 28 tests, strongest for occupancy entropy with the general factor (r = 0.20, pperm = 0.006). A novel exponential distance model reproduced the off-diagonal group transition matrix at r = 0.90, beat all 5,040 exact state-label permutations, and generalized at r = 0.54 under leave-one-participant-out cross-validation.
Supervised by Prof. Gustavo Deco & Prof. Rubén Moreno-Bote · UPF

Clinical ML
Seizure Detection - Foundation Model
Fine-tuned PatchTST for seizure detection, scored by sensitivity and false alarms per hour rather than accuracy: seizures occupy well under one percent of a long EEG recording, so a model that never predicts one is still almost always right. Trained and tested on CHB-MIT Scalp EEG from PhysioNet: 23 pediatric cases, roughly 969 hours of recording containing 198 seizures at 256 Hz across 23 channels. Deployed as a working service with FastAPI and Streamlit on AWS SageMaker.

3D U-Net for Brain Tumor Segmentation
Developed and trained a 3D U-Net for volumetric brain tumor segmentation using MRI scans from the BraTS2020 dataset. Three-dimensional convolutions learn spatial features across adjacent MRI slices rather than processing each slice independently. Optimized voxel-level overlap between predicted and ground-truth tumor regions using Dice loss, and applied data augmentation and learning-rate scheduling during training to improve generalization and stabilize optimization.

EEG → Image Decoding via CLIP
Asked whether the category of a picture someone is looking at can be read from their EEG. A Transformer encoder maps each EEG segment onto the CLIP embedding of the image that produced it, trained with an NT-Xent contrastive loss. Retrieves the three most likely image categories on THINGS-EEG2, and GPT-4o turns them into a description of what the person appeared to be seeing.

Deep Q-Network for the Beer Game
Reproduced Oroojlooyjadid et al. on the Beer Game, a four-stage supply chain where each agent sees only local information, cannot communicate, and shares one objective with the other three. Implemented the paper's reward shaping: at episode end every stored reward is adjusted by the gap between the chain's average cost and the agent's own, so the agent still acts locally but learns to lower total cost.
